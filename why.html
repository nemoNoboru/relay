The problem

Relay started as a contrarian attack against cloud providers — a piece of software with a point of view about the world baked into it. That stance is still here, but we learned something along the way: an alternative matters more than an argument.

Building useful software beats shipping an ideology with every install. To make Relay a real alternative to the platforms, we pivoted — hard — away from preaching and toward simply being a better place to be.

Relay as an alternative

Relay is a virtual third place: somewhere for you and your friends to share photos, images, ideas, articles, and links — a corner of the web that is genuinely yours. Run by someone in your group, it is as easy to set up as a Minecraft server. You have the server, and your friends — only your friends (or friends of friends, through invitations) — can post on it.

What that means in practice:

  • You own your data. Everything lives in one data directory on a computer someone in the group keeps. Back it up and you have backed up the whole node.
  • A third place for your friends. A node is sized for roughly four to twenty-nine people — not the public internet, and not a feed tuned to keep strangers scrolling.
  • A small group sharing what they like. Everyone has a page, and there is one home feed where you can see everyone's posts. Comment on your friends' posts. No retweets, no reach games.
  • No algorithms, no ads, no intervention. Nothing ranks your feed, nothing monetizes your attention, and no platform nudges anyone's politics. It is just a place for your group.

We believe that pivoting away from platform-owned social networks is, by itself, already a good change in the world. It doesn't need a slogan attached to it — you can just use it.

Host yours

Relay is one binary and one data directory. If someone in your group can keep a small server — an old laptop, a Raspberry Pi, a cheap VPS — they can run your node.

The hosting guide walks through installing and running a node, choosing an invitation, and getting your friends on board. Everything a node needs is in the technicals.
