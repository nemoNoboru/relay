The web has been captured. What began as a network for sharing what people know is now five websites, owned by five companies, hosted on three clouds. Relay is free software that any collective, club or neighbour can run: a press that can't be bought.

No accounts · No tracking · No terms that change overnight · No master

Run a node

One command

An old laptop is enough. The node starts with the password relay; open the keys page and change it, then hand out invite links.

curl -sSfL https://raw.githubusercontent.com/nemoNoboru/relay/main/install.sh | sh

relay node --name my-collective --data ~/relay --listen :8080
  1. Sign in at /_relay/admin.html with relay, then choose your own password.
  2. Make an invite for each person. They open the link, drop their site, and are live.
  3. They invite others in turn. Revoke one key and everyone they brought in is cut off too.

Five theses on the web

  1. On rent

    The big platforms live less on profit than on rent. They own the ground and take a cut of everything built on it. You bring in the harvest; they keep it.

    Y. Varoufakis, Technofeudalism, 2023

  2. On enclosure

    The web was common land. In twenty years it was fenced off by five companies, the way the fields were fenced before it.

    K. Marx, Das Kapital, part VIII, 1867

  3. On dwelling

    “Not a simple visiting right.” A place on the web should be somewhere you live and shape, not a room rented by the month.

    H. Lefebvre, Le droit à la ville, 1968

  4. On tools

    Once, anyone could write a page and put it up. Now it takes frameworks, pipelines and specialists. A convivial tool needs none of them: drop it, no account, no permission.

    I. Illich, Tools for Conviviality, 1973

  5. On the spectacle

    The feed turns life into something to watch. A site of your own is something you make, for the people around you.

    G. Debord, La société du spectacle, 1967
